# Ship generation

Run the project, or open `generated_ship.tscn` and use the editor's **F6 (Run Current Scene)**. Once playing, **F5** generates a new random layout and **F6** repeats the current seed. The HUD shows seed, room count, culling state, and separate bridge/reactor reachability checks from the airlock using the room graph. Missing destinations or destinations without a route show `unavailable`. Generation failures are reported without resetting the player. The original `map_debug.tscn` remains available for manual room/portal testing.

**K** toggles room culling immediately for visual/performance comparisons; the HUD shows its current state. With culling off, all authored rooms and lights render. Exploration and collisions are unchanged, and the culling choice survives regeneration. The inspector's `RoomVisibility.enabled` property sets the starting preference. F8 is not a game control: Godot reserves it for stopping the running project.

### Room visibility and explored map

`RoomVisibility` uses the camera's view through connected doorway apertures, not a fixed room-distance bubble. Aligned rooms stay rendered for the full visible sightline, including through closed glass doors; offscreen and around-corner branches can be hidden. Camera turns, projection changes, and doorway crossings update visibility even when the player stays in one room. Small threshold allowances prevent near-plane flashes. All room collisions, door logic, and gameplay nodes stay loaded; child light settings are preserved. Disable culling with `enabled`, or set optional `camera_path` to override the viewport's current camera. Room placement is treated as static between generations; moving or rotating the whole Ship node is supported. This is conservative doorway-based visibility, not detailed occlusion by furniture inside rooms.

Press **Tab** for the reveal-as-you-explore map. Only rooms you have entered and connections between recorded rooms are drawn; looking through doors or disabling culling does not reveal the map. Previously visited rooms stay recorded after you leave. Bridge/reactor names and locations appear only after entering those rooms. Generated in-world destination/route signs and their F7 toggle have been removed. The UI is deliberately bare-bones: a flat dark background, colored room shapes, plain lowercase labels, a white player dot with a heading line, and one line of controls. There is no frame, survey header, legend, label badge, grid, glow, or exploration/zoom dashboard. Rooms are blue, the current room green, bridge cyan, reactor orange, and stairs yellow; stairs have a simple line and up/down destination height. **PageUp/PageDown** browse discovered decks, **+/-** zoom, **Home** follows the player, and **End** fits the explored area. Gameplay continues while the map is open. A successful regeneration resets exploration after spawning; a failed generation retains the existing map.

Runtime regression: `godot --headless --path . --script res://tests/room_exploration.gd`. This checks room transitions, distant light visibility, retained floor collision, discovery memory, transformed ships, stairs/decks, culling toggles, bridge reachability status, and regeneration.

Sightline regression: `godot --headless --path . --script res://tests/room_visibility_sightlines.gd`. It exercises long visible chains, camera-only turns, clipped side branches, transparent closed doors, near-plane crossings, transformed raised doorways, and cyclic/multiple routes.

Stair modules have increased selection priority so multi-deck routes occur more frequently; every stair still has to connect to a room beyond it.

Select the generated scene's `Ship` node to configure `room_count` (default 24, including corridors) and `generation_seed` (default 0, meaning random) in the inspector. Layouts grow into a compact, irregular network of interconnected rooms and short passages, not a single corridor with side destinations. Multi-exit rooms can connect different parts of the interior; several genuine junctions split the exploration route. Compatible unused sockets that meet are joined into alternate routes. There is exactly one reachable bridge and reactor. The bridge attaches to a random valid location in the completed network, rather than the entrance junction; the same seed reproduces its location. The armory remains rare.

Corridor-to-corridor (including stair transit) joins stay open. Each room entrance uses one working instance of the existing `util/door_airlock.tscn` asset: it rises automatically when the player approaches, clears the doorway with its collision, and closes after the player leaves. Unused **room** sockets are filled with solid, flush plain walls, not decorative doors or blocked destinations. Corridors have no unused sockets or wall-capped spare arms. The level owns one shared environment.

Corridors and stairs cannot be terminal stubs: each must connect to at least two neighbors. A proposed passage is accepted only together with a room at its other end; if no room fits, the proposed passage is removed from the plan. Once all planned connections and touching-socket joins are known, corridor geometry is selected from the actual connected opening directions: two opposite openings use a straight, two perpendicular openings use an L corner, three use a T, and four use an X. Replacement prefabs are quarter-turned to preserve the connected sockets' world positions and outward directions; connection socket indices are remapped to the selected prefab. A compact straight fits the junction-sized span, while an already complete long straight retains its original span and decoration. If no authored shape fits all connected openings, that layout attempt is rejected rather than capping spare corridor arms. The requested room count, graph connections, destination doors, signs, and seeded replay are preserved.

All six corridor prefabs (`corridor`, `corridor_short`, `corridorL`, `corridorR`, `corridorT`, and `corridorX`) follow the airlock's visual design: white upper/lower bevels, recessed floor strips and wall panels, angled cool-white light panels, exposed conduits, and recessed ceiling fans. Details stop short of door thresholds and branch openings; socket locations and modular footprints remain unchanged.

To author rooms, use the existing `ship/*.tscn` prefabs as the pattern: attach `generation/ship_room.gd` to the unscaled room root, set `room_kind`, and give it a root-local `bounds: AABB` enclosing the entire solid shell, not just the walkable interior. `selection_weight` controls relative frequency among optional rooms (default 1); bridge and reactor are required rather than selected by weight. Add direct `DoorSocket` (`generation/door_socket.gd`) Marker3D children beneath a root-level `Doors` Node3D. Each socket belongs at the opening's center on the **outer shell face**, with local **-Z pointing out of the room** and local Y upright; `opening_size` is its clear width/height (default 5.4 × 3.6). Keep socket scale at 1 and leave the doorway physically open for the generator's doors or caps.

For runtime code, `$Ship.generate(seed_value)` returns success (`-1` uses the inspector seed; `0` requests randomness). Successful generation emits `generation_completed`; failures emit `generation_failed(reason)` and expose `last_error`. `$Ship.rooms: Array[ShipRoom]` holds the placed rooms, with the airlock at index 0, and `$Ship.graph: AStar3D` uses those same room indices as point IDs for connectivity/path queries, e.g. `graph.get_id_path(0, target_room_index)`. `used_seed` records the generated layout's seed, `room.get_doors()` returns its sockets, and a connected socket's `peer` identifies the matching socket. Regeneration replaces the room objects and graph; refresh stored references after `generation_completed`.

The generator includes a built-in catalog of room and passage prefabs. Add custom `ShipRoom` scenes to the Ship node's `additional_rooms` array before its first generation. The `corridor` and `stairs` room kinds identify transit. Optional multi-exit rooms can be through rooms; the airlock, bridge, and reactor remain destinations rather than forced through routes. Growth is limited to two successive transit segments and spreads across available room exits; stretched single-axis layouts are rejected. Layouts are planned before replacing the playable ship, so a failed request leaves the previous layout intact. The graph describes room connectivity, not obstacle-aware navigation inside furnished rooms.

For layouts of at least 10 rooms, the minimum junction count is `max(2, floor(room_count / 12.0))`; rounding down is explicit, rather than relying on implicit integer division.

Headless regression check: `godot --headless --path . --script res://tests/ship_generation.gd`. It covers deterministic seeds 1, 3, 12, and 42 plus random-seed replay; varying bridge locations; minimum/maximum room counts; multiple real junctions and a non-linear footprint; unique reachable bridge/reactor; reachability, non-overlap, outward aligned sockets, and flush unused room walls. Corridor checks reload each authored scene and derive its straight/corner/T/X shape from socket geometry, require its authored socket count to equal its connected degree, and reject moved, removed, or unused corridor sockets. Physical smoke checks sweep the production player's capsule from every corridor center through each connected arm while doors are open, and require solid shell collision in directions without an authored arm. Existing checks exercise every generated door's motion and collision, including automatic approach/open, physical passage, and departure/close behavior.
